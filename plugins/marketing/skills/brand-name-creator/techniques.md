# Naming techniques (Lexicon Branding method)

Reference for step 4 of the brand-name-creator skill. Each section is a generation
direction; the recipes at the end are how to reach 100 names per direction without
stopping to judge.

## Sound symbolism: the energy of letters

Every letter carries a feeling before the word means anything. Seed lists with the
letter whose energy matches the experience defined in step 2.

| Letter | Energy | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| V | The most alive, vibrant sound in English | Vercel, Viagra, Corvette |
| B | Reliability and trust. Anchored BlackBerry when the keyboard design made the client skeptical | BlackBerry |
| Z | Noisy, high signal, stands out in a crowded market | Azure: soft A start, Z in the middle, smooth flow at the end |
| X | Fast and crisp, strong association with innovation | Xbox, SpaceX |

General phonosemantics beyond those four: plosives (P, T, K, D, G) feel quick and
energetic; fricatives (S, F, Z, V) feel smooth and fast; liquids and nasals (L, M, N, R)
feel warm and calm; front vowels (i, e) feel small, light and quick; back vowels (o, u)
feel big, heavy and slow.

## Processing fluency

The brain is lazy and rejects complex input. Great names put simple, familiar blocks
together so the brain leans in. Vercel is coined, yet made of easy roots: "ver" (truth,
as in in vino veritas; green, as in verde) plus "cel" (acceleration).

Checks: two or three syllables; spellable after hearing it once; pronounceable in the
target market's languages (English and Portuguese for Brazil); no consonant pile-ups.

## Association multipliers: compounds

Two distinct words act as a multiplier of associations. Powerbook, Facebook, Windsurf:
1 + 1 = 3, because "wind" and "surf" each fire their own images in parallel. Recipe: one
word from the product experience or feeling, one word from an outside world (step 3), and
both images must fire.

## Structural play

Sonos is a palindrome: perfectly balanced, reads the same forward and backward, still
readable flipped upside down. Try palindromes, mirrored syllables (so-no-s), doubled
letters, and strong rhythm (da-DA, DA-da-da). Letters that survive a flip: o, s, x, z,
n/u, p/d, b/q.

## Make the intangible tangible

Builders drown in process names (Windsurf was first called Codium). Ground software in
concrete physical experience: movement, flow, sport, wind, water, tools, terrain. Ask what
the product feels like in the body, then name that.

## Start a story, not a statement

Cloud Pro states what the product is and disappears into the ocean of competitors. Azure
starts a story, which buys an asymmetric advantage and room to grow. Test: does the name
describe, or does it make a stranger ask a question?

## Generation recipes

- Root matrix: 10 roots from the product experience × 10 roots from an outside world
  gives 100 compounds and blends. Keep every cell.
- Letter seeding: pick a letter's energy, list 30 outside-world words starting with it,
  then clip, blend and respell each.
- Clip and fuse: first syllable of one word plus last syllable of another (ver + cel).
- Respell for signal: c to k, s to z, i to y, drop a vowel, double a consonant. One change
  per name; keep it pronounceable.
- Borrow: Latin, Greek, Portuguese, Italian, Japanese words for the physical metaphor.
  Check meaning and slang in the target market before shortlisting.
- Palindromes and mirrors: build from syllable pairs, then check the flip.

## Review by speculating

Per name, ask: What could we do with this? What is the story? How would we execute it
(logo, tagline, product line, tone of voice)? Never "Do we like it?".
